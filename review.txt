(한글 제목) 한국: 어른에게 술 따르는 예절 #Shorts
후크: Ever seen a Korean pour a drink with two hands and wonder why?
본문: In Korean drinking etiquette, pouring for someone older or more senior is a way to show respect. A person commonly holds the bottle with the right hand while supporting the right forearm or wrist with the left hand. The person receiving the drink may also hold the glass with both hands or support it with the other hand. This custom is especially associated with soju and other shared drinks, though the level of formality depends on age, relationship, and setting. Among close friends of similar age, a more casual one-handed pour may be acceptable.
반전: It is also considered polite to receive a drink from an elder with both hands, often with a slight bow.

=== [23] 100‑Day Celebration ===
(사실 확인용 검색어) Baek-il Korean 100 day baby celebration, baekseolgi rice cake Korea, Korean baby 100 days tradition
(한글 제목) 한국: 아기의 백일 축하 #Shorts
후크: Did you know Korean families often celebrate a baby’s 100th day?
본문: In South Korea, many families celebrate a baby’s 100th day, called “baek-il.” The celebration often includes a simple family gathering and foods such as baekseolgi, a plain white steamed rice cake, and seaweed soup. Traditionally, baekseolgi was shared with others in the hope that blessings and good health would return to the baby. The day is a meaningful family milestone, though how families celebrate it varies widely today. Some keep the ceremony simple at home, while others take commemorative photos or hold a small gathering.
반전: Baek-il is different from doljanchi, the larger first-birthday celebration held when a baby turns one year old.
